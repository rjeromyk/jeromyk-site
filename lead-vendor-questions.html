A Lead Buyer's Checklist

Before You Buy Leads, Ask These 5 Questions

Any lead vendor can make promises. A trustworthy vendor should be able to show the records behind them.

1

Where did this lead come from?

"Facebook" or "online" is not enough. Ask the vendor to identify the actual source path:

  • →Campaign or traffic source
  • →Ad or creative
  • →Landing page or form
  • →Capture flow
  • →Source and tracking data

What you're looking for: traceability—not a vague explanation.

2

How many agents received this lead?

A vendor calling a lead "exclusive" does not prove exclusivity. Ask whether they can show:

  • →Who received the lead
  • →When it was assigned
  • →Whether another buyer received a copy
  • →The order or account connected to the assignment

What you're looking for: assignment records showing exactly where the lead went.

3

Where is the consumer's consent record?

If the consumer opted in, there should be evidence tied to the capture. Ask for:

  • →Consent timestamp
  • →Form and source information
  • →Consent language
  • →TrustedForm certificate or other consent evidence, where available

What you're looking for: a verifiable consent trail—not simply "all our leads opt in."

4

What is the credit or replacement policy?

You should understand the rules before purchasing—not after filing a complaint. Ask:

  • →What qualifies for a credit or replacement?
  • →What does not qualify?
  • →How long do you have to report a problem?
  • →What evidence is required?
  • →How is the request reviewed?

What you're looking for: a written policy in plain English and a trackable review process.

5

How quickly was the lead delivered?

"Real-time" should be supported by timestamps. Ask the vendor to show:

  • →When the consumer submitted the form
  • →When the lead was created
  • →When it was assigned
  • →When delivery was attempted
  • →When the buyer actually received it

What you're looking for: the complete capture → assignment → delivery timeline.
